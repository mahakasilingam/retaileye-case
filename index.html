RetailEye: the term sheet that decides who owns the shelf data

A Shanghai computer-vision start-up has two Series A offers and very little time. Which one should it take, and what should it push back on?

Retail AIVenture financingNegotiation

The decision in one paragraph

By early 2021, RetailEye, founded in 2017 by CEO Fan Lingyun and CTO Yuan Hongliang, had cash for only a few more months. It was seeking ¥30–40 million to keep its algorithms competitive and to win more customers. Two venture firms, Earlybird Wealth and Gamma Ventures, sent term sheets. The founders had to decide fast whether to accept one or negotiate.

The central question: how should RetailEye choose and negotiate its Series A while supporting rapid growth and protecting long-term value?

Why a shelf photo is worth money

Brands write detailed display rules, but they cannot see whether stores follow them. Without store-level transparency, a brand cannot tell why sales rise or fall. In China’s beverage market, where most buying happens offline and on impulse, this gap is costly.

From unstructured images to structured data

RetailEye began with Swire Coca-Cola, whose inspectors manually photographed shelves and analysts checked each SKU. The founders replaced that chain with computer vision. It is a clean example of the big-data value chain: volume (millions of images), variety (SKUs, displays, promotions), velocity (reports in seconds) and veracity (it can even flag reused photos as fraud).

Manual:Store visit→Human inspection→Photos→Manual analysis→Delayed report
RetailEye:Store image→AI recognition→Data extraction→Automated analysis→Real-time insight

Dashboard: what the AI changed at Swire Coca-Cola

Competitors raised the stakes: Trax had raised over US$1 billion in total and served 90 countries, and ImageDT claimed 99.8% accuracy in China. Standing still meant losing the data race, which is why the money was urgent.

The hidden clause: liquidation preference

Gamma wins on seven of eight rows, but the one it loses carries the most weight. If the company is sold, Gamma takes 1.5 times its money first and then shares in what is left. Move the slider to see how much of an exit each structure takes for the Series A investor.

At the case example of a ¥100M exit, Gamma’s terms pay it about ¥64.31M (¥60M + 10.78% × ¥40M). A plain 1× non-participating term caps it at ¥40M until the exit passes roughly ¥371M, the post-money valuation, where converting to shares becomes better.

What to accept, what to counter

Accept from Gamma

  • ¥40M investment
  • ¥331.06M pre-money
  • 10.78% Series A equity
  • 1% dividend, 6% redemption IRR
  • Broad-based anti-dilution
  • Balanced board: 2 investor, 2 founder, 1 independent
  • 1-year non-compete

Counter-negotiate

  • Liquidation: 1.5× participating → 1× non-participating (the priority)
  • Exclusivity: 60 → 45 days, to keep other options open
  • Keep 6% redemption, with reasonable conditions

The objective is not to reject Gamma, but to rebalance the risk-sharing terms.

The data lens: why the money matters beyond the cap table

Capital buys a data flywheel. More customers send more store images, which give more structured data and better models. Better models produce better retail insight, which raises customer value and attracts more customers. Each turn moves RetailEye from an AI inspection tool towards a retail-intelligence platform.

The advantage then depends on data quality, model accuracy, governance, ownership and integration, not on collecting more images alone.

Conclusion

Choose Gamma Ventures, subject to renegotiating the liquidation preference. It offers more capital (¥40M), a higher valuation (¥331.06M pre-money), less dilution (10.78%), slightly more founder ownership (27.84%), a lower dividend and redemption return, gentler anti-dilution and a shorter non-compete. Its only serious weakness is the 1.5× participating preference, which could hand the investor a disproportionate share of a modest exit.

The decision is not about the highest valuation. It is about balancing capital, dilution, investor protection, founder control and future value.

One practical note beyond the deck: if Gamma refuses a full 1× non-participating term, a middle path (for example 1× participating with a cap, or 1.5× non-participating) would still be a big improvement over the original draft. Fall back to it only after the first counter.
